# DFlash2 paired n=250 quality gate — PASSED (2026-08-20, PM pre-night2)

**Status:** measured (2026-08-20 PM, GB10/sm121). Companion to
[`dflash2-sm121-first-light.md`](dflash2-sm121-first-light.md); this closes
the "paired n=250 gate" that was left open there.

## TL;DR

The paired n=250 gate **PASSES**. DFlash2 speculative decoding (FP8-KV,
block-diffusion drafter, num_speculative_tokens=15) is **quality-neutral**
vs. the non-speculative NVFP4-KV production canon at gate tier, and finished
the identical GSM8K workload **2.53× faster** in wall-clock at concurrency 4.
The n=15 first-light 94/100 was noise, as suspected.

| Arm | GSM8K n=250 (greedy) | Determinism 3×2 (single-stream) | tok/s single (count-200) | GSM wall-clock, c=4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B — Baseline NVFP4-KV (prod, non-spec) | **242/250 = 96.8%** | all identical | 10.8 | 3016 s |
| A — DFlash2 spec, FP8-KV (n=15) | **239/250 = 95.6%** | all identical | 93.6 (accepted) | **1193 s (2.53×)** |

- **McNemar exact, two-sided: p = 0.375** — discordant b=4 (base-only correct),
  c=1 (dflash-only correct). No significant quality difference; the 3 net
  flips are the batch-numerics class (greedy verified spec-decode cannot move
  the output distribution — the drafter only changes acceptance rate).
  Gate rule PASS: p ≥ 0.05 ∧ acc_A ≥ acc_B − 0.02 (0.956 ≥ 0.948).
- **No confounding:** both arms served with `--reasoning-parser qwen3` +
  `--enable-auto-tool-choice --tool-call-parser qwen3_coder` (the lesson from
  the CB-eval). Verified on the live prod container and in `debug-boot.sh`.

## Reading the numbers

- **Quality:** the gate confirms adoption reasoning for the interactive
  latency tier is now backed by a passed quality gate, not just the n=15
  hint.
- **tok/s caveat:** the 93.6 tok/s single-stream is the **count-to-200**
  probe — a highly predictable sequence, best case for the drafter (peak
  accept-length 5.37). It is not the general-traffic number. Over the n=250
  GSM run the server SpecDecoding metrics show mean acceptance length
  ~4.2–5.4 → effective ~26–31 tok/s as in first-light. Honest headline:
  *equal quality, ~2.5–3× single-stream latency.*
- **Concurrency surprise worth a follow-up:** at c=4 on GSM, DFlash2 finished
  in 40% of baseline wall-clock (1193 vs 3016 s) — i.e. *faster* under this
  modest concurrency, whereas first-light found it slower under the
  ab_battery parallel phase. The difference is load level/shape (c=4 uniform
  GSM vs. higher/mixed concurrency). The clean batch sweep (1/2/4/8) is still
  the right next probe before any fleet-wide claim.
- **Determinism caveat:** both arms were byte-identical on 3×2 greedy
  single-stream. One of the three probes (alphabet-reverse) returned **empty
  content** both times on both arms — a reasoning-mode swallow (final content
  empty, thinking not surfaced), degenerate but consistent; determinism claim
  holds, but pick less think-prone determinism prompts next time.
